# Research Agenda

## Primary hypothesis
Verified synthetic and counterfactual experience can reduce the number of real user interactions required for useful personalization while preserving calibration and robustness under preference drift.

## H1 — Sparse learning
A personalized agent can reach a target utility level with substantially fewer real interactions than retrieval-only or real-only adaptation.

## H2 — Counterfactual generalization
Counterfactual experiences improve performance on unseen contexts more than matched-volume non-counterfactual synthetic data.

## H3 — Selection efficiency
Uncertainty-, failure-, or information-gain-driven selection outperforms random synthetic-data selection at fixed training budget.

## H4 — Calibrated adaptation
Explicit confidence and temporal state reduce high-confidence incorrect personalization and stale-preference errors.

## Negative/control hypothesis
More personalization is not always better. On irrelevant tasks, a strong system should refrain from injecting user-specific information.
